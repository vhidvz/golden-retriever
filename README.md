# Buddy

A Golden Retriever who lives on a Linux desktop. He is born as a puppy, grows
with real time, and keeps living when every AI service is unavailable.

This repository is at **Phase 5 (skills and helpers)**: on top of a life of his
own, senses that each have their own switch, and an understanding of English
and Persian that he can be trained in, he can now do things on the computer
when asked, offer to help when he sees a habit of yours begin, and call on a
local model. Phase 6 (refinement) has begun with his body: one
continuous, skinned, furred mesh generated by a script in this repository.

## What is here

| Part | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Brain | `crates/buddy-core` | Identity, age, needs, mood, temperament, behaviour selection, memory (SQLite), and a long-run simulator. No graphics, no models. |
| Daemon and CLI | `crates/buddy` | Runs the brain, serves a Unix socket, supervises the renderer. |
| Renderer | `renderer/` | Godot 4 project: a transparent, click-through overlay window, a fur-covered placeholder dog animated procedurally, and his recorded sounds. |
| Skills | `crates/buddy/src/skills.rs`, `daemon/assist.rs`, `ai.rs` | What he can do on the computer, the permissions around it, and the optional local model (Ollama). |
| Senses | `shell-extension/`, `perception/`, `crates/buddy/src/system.rs` | What he perceives: a GNOME Shell extension for the desktop, small helpers for screen, camera and microphone, and a poller for idle time, lock and load. |
| Service | `dist/buddy.service`, `scripts/install.sh` | Starts him with the graphical session, as the user, without root. |
| Evidence | `spikes/` | The measurements the design rests on, with the harnesses that produced them. |

The daemon owns everything about him. The renderer only draws the state it is
sent, so it can crash, be hidden or be replaced without him noticing.

## Run it

Requirements: Rust and an X11 or XWayland display. The installer downloads a pinned
Godot 4.7.2 under `~/.local/share/buddy/godot` unless a suitable `godot`/`godot4` is on
`PATH` or `BUDDY_GODOT` is set. Developed on Manjaro with GNOME 50.

```sh
cargo test                      # brain and daemon tests
cargo run -- daemon             # run from the checkout
scripts/install.sh              # install for this user and start with the session
scripts/install.sh --remove     # stop and remove (his memory is kept)
```

```sh
buddy status            # how he is, what is running, what is being observed
buddy hide [MINUTES]    # stops the renderer entirely; `buddy show` brings him back
buddy why               # what he is doing, why, how he feels, what else he considered
buddy sleep | wake
buddy feed | water | praise | treat
buddy say "بادی بشین"    # say something to him, in English or Persian
buddy teach "bang" roll_over   # tell him outright what a word means
buddy knows             # the words he knows, how well, and how he came by them
buddy unlearn "bang"
buddy memories          # what he was told to remember; `buddy forget N` removes one

buddy say "find my tax report"      # ask for something on the computer
buddy waiting | approve | deny      # what he wants a yes for, and your answer
buddy skills                        # what he can do, and whether he asks first
buddy workflows                     # the application routines he has recorded
buddy workflow record NAME          # record the applications you work through, then stop
buddy workflow capture NAME         # record accessible actions inside applications
buddy workflow capture-step         # capture the focused button/menu item
buddy workflow stop | cancel
buddy workflow replay NAME|ID       # replay it, checking each step worked
buddy folders add ~/Documents       # where he may search, read and move files
buddy undo | result                 # take back his last change; see his last output
buddy providers                     # is the local model there?
buddy ai status                     # how Buddy's managed Ollama runtime is doing
buddy ai setup                      # recommend and install a model for this machine
buddy ai model [NAME]               # show or change the selected model
buddy ai doctor | repair            # diagnose the runtime / re-pull the selected model
buddy quiet on|off      # no sounds and no asking for attention
buddy privacy on|off    # suspends every sense at once
buddy senses            # what he may perceive, and what is actually reporting
buddy sense screen on   # switch one sense: system, desktop, window_titles, screen, camera, microphone, accessibility
buddy look              # have him look at the screen once and say what he makes of it
buddy set adult_days 30 # re-time growth without resetting him
buddy journal | export  # what happened; everything he knows, as JSON
buddy simulate 90 --user absent   # live 90 days offline and report how varied they were
```

On the dog: click to pet, drag to move, right-click for a menu.

`scripts/install.sh` also installs a pinned Ollama runtime under
`~/.local/share/buddy/ollama` and starts it with `buddy-ollama.service` on
`127.0.0.1:11435`. It never uses or changes a system Ollama on 11434; `--no-ai`
installs Buddy without a runtime or model. The first-run model recommendation is
`buddy ai setup`. Models whose licence is not a known permissive one require
`--accept-license` after you have read the licence shown by the recommendation.

## How he decides

Seven needs (fatigue, hunger, thirst, boredom, company, itch, stiffness) drift
with time and with what he does. A mood of two numbers, how pleasant and how
activated, follows from them and from what happens to him. Nine temperament
traits, set at birth and shifting slowly with how he is treated, weight
everything.

Each of his 19 activities scores itself against that state. Scores are damped
by how recently he did the same thing, and the choice is drawn in proportion to
them, so the most fitting thing usually happens without anything being certain.
Every instance gets its own variant, duration, vigour and random seed, which
the renderer turns into a pose, quick motions on top, and a mood layer (tail,
ears, mouth, eyes). Nothing is a recorded clip.

Unmet needs lower his mood only mildly, an absence leaves him rested and glad
to see you, and an unanswered bid for attention makes him wait twice as long
before the next. He is never used to pressure the user.

Now and then he looks up at whoever is in front of the screen. Without a
camera that is taken to be the middle of the monitor, a little above him.

His memory lives in `~/.local/share/buddy/buddy.db`, with a daily backup beside
it that is restored automatically if the database is ever damaged.

## What he perceives

| Sense | Default | What it tells him | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| `system` | on | Seconds since you touched the computer, screen lock, system load | Session bus and `/proc`, polled every 2 s |
| `desktop` | on | Which application is in front, fullscreen, overview, pointer position, that a notification arrived | A GNOME Shell extension |
| `window_titles` | off | Window titles as well | The same extension |
| `screen` | off | A one-line description of what is on screen, when asked to look | One frame through the desktop portal, described by a local model |
| `camera` | off | Whether someone is there | Movement in a 64×48 greyscale picture; no frame is kept |
| `microphone` | off | That there was a sound, and how loud | Loudness only; nothing is recorded |
| `accessibility` | off | The focused control's app, frame, role and accessible name, only while capturing a workflow | AT-SPI on demand; no text, keys or coordinates |

A sense that is off is not consulted at all: the extension is told to send
nothing, the camera and microphone helpers are not running, and the accessibility
helpers are only started for a capture or replay step. Privacy mode
does the same for all of them at once. `buddy senses` shows what is allowed and
what has actually reported; the journal records when each sense first became
active and each look at the screen. A look keeps only its one-line summary, in
memory; the frame is deleted as soon as the model has seen it, and nothing
leaves the machine.

What he does with it: he greets you when you come back, steps aside for the
lock screen, the overview and fullscreen applications, glances up at a
notification, watches the pointer when it comes near, stays calmer and does
not ask for attention while you are busy, and takes less of the machine when
it is working hard. He also keeps count of which applications follow which,
and notes a sequence as a habit of yours once it has recurred on different
days (`buddy why` lists them). Acting on those habits is a later phase.

## Talking to him and training him

He never answers in words. What you say changes what he does.

**Understanding** happens in three steps, each optional beyond the first:

1. His own vocabulary: `crates/buddy-core/data/lexicon.json` lists every phrase
   he knows, per language, and what the user has taught him is checked before it.
   Text is normalised first, so Persian letter variants, the half-space and
   punctuation make no difference. This needs no model.
2. A local language model through Ollama (`gemma4:e4b` unless changed with
   `buddy set language_model …`), for what the vocabulary cannot place, such as
   "I really don't want you to do that anymore".
3. If neither can, he looks at you and tilts his head.

**Training.** Each word has a strength for each trick. Hearing a word, he does
the strongest trick with a probability that grows with its strength and falls
when he is tired or over-excited, so an untrained dog obeys only sometimes.
What follows within twenty seconds teaches him:

| You | Effect |
|---|---|
| "Good boy" / «آفرین», a treat, petting | Strengthens the word-to-trick pairing he just acted on |
| "That's right" / «درسته» | Strengthens it a little |
| "No, that's wrong" / «نه، اشتباهه» | Weakens that pairing, and he stops |
| "Stop that" / «بسه» | He stops and will not start that activity again for half an hour |
| "When I say X, do Y" / «وقتی می‌گم X، یعنی Y» | Sets the pairing outright |
| A new word, then praise while he happens to do something | He comes to pair the word with that |
| "Remember that …" / «یادت باشه …» | Kept word for word in his memory |

`buddy knows` shows every pairing with its strength, whether it is instinct,
told or learned, and how often it was rewarded and corrected; `buddy unlearn`
removes one. What he was told to remember is listed by `buddy memories`. The
journal records what kind of thing was said to him, never the words: talk does
not become memory unless he is told to remember it. One thing he is told
already changes how he lives: say you work late and his night starts two hours later.

**By voice.** With the microphone sense on, stretches of speech are written
down by the local model on this machine and the recording is deleted at once.
He acts on them only if his name is in them, or within twenty seconds of being
spoken to by name. This path has been tested with a recording, not with a
microphone, and not yet in Persian.

## Doing things on the computer

He never acts on the computer except through eight skills, each a small piece
of local code with its own limits:

| Skill | What it does | By default |
|---|---|---|
| `find_files`, `read_file` | Look in the folders he was given | Allowed |
| `open_app`, `open_file`, `make_note`, `move_file` | Small or reversible changes | Asks first |
| `run_command` | Runs a shell command | Always asks, unless that exact command was approved for good |
| `replay_workflow` | Replays a saved application routine and checks each step | Asks first |

**How a request is worked out.** "Open X", "find X", "note X" and their Persian
forms are placed by his own vocabulary, with no model. Anything else goes to
the local model, which picks one skill and its arguments. The model only ever
*chooses*; `skills.rs` is the only code that touches the system.

**Permission.** Where a skill asks first, he sits and waits. Answer with
`buddy approve` / `buddy deny`, or just say yes or no to him. `--always` makes
a yes standing for that skill (never for `run_command`); `--never`
makes a no final. He has no folders until given some (`buddy folders add`), and
a path that leads out of them is refused. A note and a move can be taken back
with `buddy undo`. The journal records what he did and whether it worked, not
its output.

**His own offers.** When the first two applications of a habit he has noticed
come to the front, he gets up, faces you, bounces and gives a soft woof: he
would like to bring up the third. `buddy waiting` says what he has in mind.
Yes, and he does it; "always do that", and next time he does not ask; "not
now", and he lets it go; "don't help me with this", or three refusals, and he
never offers that again. An offer nobody answers lapses after ninety seconds.
He offers at most once in ten minutes, never to an empty room, and never in
quiet mode.

**Helper.** `buddy providers` shows what is available. The local model is
optional and can fail on its own: *Ollama*, installed and managed by Buddy, is
used for understanding what vocabulary cannot place, choosing a skill,
describing a look at the screen, and writing down speech. `buddy ai setup`
checks the machine, recommends a model and installs it; only `gemma4:e4b` has
been measured by this project so far.

Without it, he still lives, obeys the words he knows, and does what his own
vocabulary can work out.

## Ground rules

- **Local first.** His life never depends on a language model or a network.
- **No paid assets or services.** Only free, openly licensed material. Anything
  under a non-commercial licence is offered as a separate optional download and
  never bundled here.
- **He is a dog.** He barks, whines, wags and looks; he never speaks with a
  human voice.
- **English and Persian from the start.** Nothing language-specific is hard-coded.
- **Camera and microphone are optional.** Every perception source has an
  "unavailable" state and its own permission.

## His body

`tools/build_dog.py` builds the dog in Blender from nothing but numbers. Each
part of him (trunk, neck, skull, jaw, a leg, the tail, an ear) is described the
way a boat is drawn: a row of cross-sections along a path, measured from
photographs of real Golden Retrievers standing in profile. Blender melts the
parts into one surface, cuts the eye sockets and nostrils, and thins the mesh
where it is flat. The script then works out, for every point of that surface,
which bones move it, how long and what gold the coat is there, which way it
lies, where there is bare skin instead (nose, lips, eye rims, pads, the inside
of the mouth) and how much light reaches it. Nothing is drawn by hand, bought,
or taken from another model:

    blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/build_dog.py
    blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/build_dog.py -- --preview /tmp/dog   # also plain pictures of the bare body

It takes a few seconds and writes `renderer/dog/retriever.json`. That file is in
the repository, so Blender is only needed to change the dog, never to run him.

His proportions are measured against breed standards, judges' material and limb
studies, and against photographs of real Goldens, in `docs/proportions.md`; the
measuring is reproducible with `python3 tools/measure_dog.py` (add `--age puppy`, or
`--picture out.png` to see where each landmark was found).

`renderer/retriever.gd` loads the mesh onto a 25-bone skeleton. The coat is
drawn by `renderer/coat.gdshaderinc` as up to 26 soft layers lifted off the skin
(a short coat gets two or three, the feathering all of them). At his size on
the screen a single hair is thinner than a pixel, so it does not draw strands:
it draws what the eye sees of a real coat at that size, which is locks lying
along the body, a sheen that follows them, darker depths and a soft outline.

The behaviour code still thinks in poses and motions; each frame those are
copied onto the bones, and each leg is solved from where its paw should be.
Paws stay flat on the ground and lift through a stride; when the hips come down
a hind leg's long foot lies along the ground, which is what lets him sit and lie
as a dog does. The jaw is its own piece, so the mouth really opens.

A puppy is not a small adult, so the model holds the body twice: as the adult,
and as a puppy of eight weeks with a short round body close to the ground,
short thick legs, a big head with a short muzzle, a stub of a tail and an even
fluff with no feathering. Both have the same points and bones; one smooth rule
in the script (`as_puppy`) carries each of them from the adult's place to the
puppy's, and the renderer moves everything between the two as he grows, a
little at a time, along with the paler coat.

The eyes sit in sockets and have lids: a blink, a sleepy droop and sleep all close
them over the eye rather than flattening it. Lying on his side, the legs go
slack and the two of the upper flank come down to rest on the floor.

Drawn live like this he is a stylised dog, not a photograph of one: the coat is
painted layers, there are no teeth, and a scratching hind paw comes up beside the
shoulder rather than reaching the neck.

### Pictures with a real coat

A real coat is far beyond what his graphics card can draw live, so the realistic
look is drawn beforehand, the way games such as Factorio make theirs. Godot runs
the very same dog code headless and records how every bone moves, frame by frame
(`renderer/export_poses.gd`); Blender rebuilds his body at the same age from the
model file, grows a coat of individual hairs on it (about 230,000 on the puppy;
length, direction, colour and the hang of the long coat all come from the model),
gives him eyes with an iris and a wet cornea and a leather nose, moves everything
with the bones and renders each frame in Cycles through exactly the camera his
window has (`tools/bake_sprites.py`).

The daemon does this by itself, and only while you are away: it starts when the
screen is locked or nothing has been touched for ten minutes, is paused the moment
you are back (the drawing process is stopped, not killed, and carries on later),
and runs at the lowest priority. His growth is divided into nine stages; the
pictures for the stage he is in are drawn first, then those for the next, so they
are ready before he gets there. A stage takes a few hours. When the body model or
the drawing script changes, every picture is drawn again, and the old ones stay in
use until the new ones are complete. When only the way he moves changes (a new
movement, a changed one), the set is brought up to date in place: each movement's
recorded poses carry a fingerprint, and only movements whose poses changed, or that
are new, are drawn again. `buddy status` says how far it has got.

    buddy set pictures away       # the default
    buddy set pictures always     # also while you are here (at the lowest priority)
    buddy set pictures off

Pictures go to `~/.local/share/buddy/sprites/`. The renderer (`renderer/sprites.gd`)
shows them in place of the 3D body whenever there are pictures of what he is doing;
the 3D dog keeps moving unseen underneath and still decides his posture, stride
and where he looks, and the matching pictures are picked: a walk, trot or gallop
by his pace and where his legs are in the stride, one of five drawn looks by where
his attention is, a stretch, a yawn, shaking off or a roll by how far through it he
is, a tail chase by how far round he has turned, a scratch or a lick as one quick
repetition going round, a drawn change when he goes from standing to sitting, lying
or sleeping on his side and back, and otherwise a stretch of the movement played
forwards and back. Each clip belongs to a posture, so going from sitting to shaking
off, say, first plays the drawn change from sitting to standing. Clips crossfade into each other, facing left
is the mirror image (light included), and his bowl is drawn into the pictures of
him eating and drinking. A movement is used as soon as all its pictures exist.

Drawing a frame this way costs about 0.12 ms of the graphics card, against 2.5 to
3.5 ms for the live body. Drawing the pictures costs about 10 seconds each on the
processor, roughly 1,700 for one stage of his growth (about 40 MB).

What is drawn: standing, sitting, lying (three ways, one on his side), sleeping
(three ways), the walk, trot and gallop, eating, drinking, sniffing the ground and
the air, the play bow, two stretches, yawning standing, sitting or lying, shaking
off, rolling on his back, scratching with either hind leg, licking a paw or his
flank, chasing his tail either way round, greeting, both ways of inviting you to
let him help, asking for attention (with and without a paw offered), looking in
five directions while standing, sitting or lying, greeting with a whole turn and a
bow, the quick turn that starts one way of inviting you, and the changes between
standing, sitting, lying and sleeping on his side. Every activity he has is drawn.

## Known gaps

- Everything he does has pictures with a real coat; until they are drawn for his
  age, he is the stylised live body (see "Pictures with a real coat"). Pictures are drawn only while the computer is left
  alone, about five hours for each stage of his growth; on a computer that is
  switched off whenever its user leaves, they may take days to appear.
- In the pictures he looks in one of five drawn directions, not anywhere at all.
- When the graphics card runs out of memory (other programs had 1.8 of its 2 GB
  during testing), a frame can come out empty; this showed up as occasional blank
  renders in the self-test and has not been made robust yet.
- His sounds are recordings of real dogs (barks, a grunt, whines, yawns, sniffs,
  shaking off, eating and drinking; see `renderer/sounds/SOURCES.md`); panting,
  growling and a puppy's yawn have none yet.
- No toys yet, so no fetch.
- The desktop sense needs GNOME; on other desktops he still has the system sense.
- The managed Ollama runtime is Linux/systemd only. The model catalogue marks
  unevaluated models; only `gemma4:e4b` has measurements from this project.
- Looking at the screen is on request only (`buddy look`), sees the whole monitor
  at reduced size, and has no OCR, so small text is beyond it.
- The camera and microphone senses detect presence and loudness only, and have
  been tested with synthetic input, not real devices.
- Habits are noticed at the level of application order, not of what is done in them.
- Speech has been tested with a recording only; there is no wake word, so with the
  microphone on every stretch of speech goes through the local model.
- "Fetch" is not among his tricks yet, because there is no ball.
- He records and replays application-level routines and, with the accessibility sense
  on, focused accessible actions inside applications. He does not yet type text, use
  password fields or terminals, or replay apps without an accessibility tree; mixed
  app-plus-inside-app workflows are rejected for now. He still has no way to press
  arbitrary keys or click coordinates for you.
- Suspend is handled by time accounting only; there is no lock or sleep signal yet.
- The renderer needs Godot; the installer provides a pinned copy, but the renderer is
  not yet exported as a standalone binary.

2. Autonomous life — done.
